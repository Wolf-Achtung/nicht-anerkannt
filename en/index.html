Manifesto

For young people who don't fit into camps

A thinking space for young people who don't fit into camps and still want to act.

We live in a time when everything is simultaneously available: knowledge, opinions, outrage, tutorials, ideologies, identities, AI output, life plans. Everything is there. Everything speaks. Everything demands attention. And almost nothing helps you truly discern.

Traditional forms of education often act as if the problem were still a lack of information. As if you just needed enough material, enough exams, enough certificates, enough content. But that era is over. The real problem is not that we know too little. The real problem is that we live amidst too much material and have too few places where it becomes judgment, conviction, and action.

Background

More on this — if you want to read deeper

The manifesto stands on its own. But if you want to know why we think this way, here are five short chapters.

1. The Problem — The False Alternative

Young people today are often presented with a false choice: either conformity or tribal thinking. Either resume optimization, functional compliance, and marketable self-presentation. Or ideological overheating, reflexive enemy images, and the comfortable relief of no longer having to think for yourself.

Both are too small. Conformity makes you useful but not alive. The camp makes you passionate but not free.

You don't have to choose between cynicism and fanaticism.

The Atelier
2. Radical Middle — Not a tepid middle. A thinking middle.

The Radical Middle is not the dull somewhere-in-between. It is not cowardly equidistance between truth and lies. It is a stance that is radical because it must think where others merely react.

It connects without diluting. It judges without rushing to conclusions. It acts without waiting for complete certainty.

Radically committed to the rule of lawFreedom without form tips into arbitrariness.
Radically dialogicalNo one alone is wise enough for the present.
Radically reality-capableIdeologies are often just shelters from the real.
Radically ready to actAnalysis without consequence is a stylistic device.
3. The Atelier — Not clinging to preconceived opinions.

The Atelier does not tell you what to think. It is a place where you learn to think better, question deeper, and act more decisively. It begins with questions that no one in the room can immediately resolve.

We work with AI, but not as an oracle. AI is a sparring partner, a counter-voice, and a stress test. Those who use AI only to produce output become replaceable. Those who use AI to think better become dangerous to every form of intellectual inertia.

4. What Emerges — Judgment instead of opinion ownership.

We train people who can think and act under uncertainty.

People who don't immediately fall into a camp when things get complicated. People who can endure contradiction without going soft. People who can use technology without being used by it.

5. The Future of Education — Protecting Firstness in the age of AI.

In the age of AI, the task of education shifts radically. No longer does the reproduction of knowledge form its core, but the cultivation of that human dimension from which genuine novelty grows: Firstness -- wonder, irritation, pre-propositional perception.

The human cultivates Firstness. AI amplifies Thirdness. The dialogue produces productive Secondness.

The Atelier is the institutional form that corresponds to this insight: an epistemic architecture in which ambiguities remain productive and the not-yet-sayable is brought into an initial form.
